Most houseplants die of scheduling, not neglect.

Watering on a calendar ignores the two variables that actually matter: how fast your soil dries, and how much light your room really gets. Both change week to week, season to season, and neither is visible from the surface of the pot.

Terra One measures both, forty-eight times a day.

01 — Sense

Four sensors, half-hourly.

Two capacitive probes track soil moisture at different depths, so Terra One sees the whole drying curve rather than a single point. An ambient light sensor and a combined temperature/humidity sensor record the conditions around the leaves.

Readings are taken every thirty minutes and stored on the device, whether or not it can reach your network.

02 — Act

A closed loop, not a timer.

A 1.2-litre reservoir sits beneath the soil column, feeding a diaphragm pump driven directly from the board. When moisture crosses your plant's threshold, the pump delivers a measured dose, then waits and re-reads the soil to confirm the water actually landed where it should.

A replaceable nutrient cartridge doses feed alongside the water during the growing season, and can be switched off entirely.

Setup

Ten minutes, once.

Pot your plant

Transfer your plant into Terra One and fill the reservoir. The sensor probes are pre-installed and pre-calibrated — nothing to assemble.

Pair over Bluetooth

Choose your species in the app. Terra One loads the matching moisture and light profile, then joins your Wi-Fi network if you want remote access.

Leave it alone

It waters on its own from there. You'll hear from it when the reservoir runs low — for most plants, about once a month.
