The problem

Houseplants rarely die of neglect. They die of bad timing.

A watering schedule assumes every week is the same. It isn't. Soil dries faster in a sunny July than in a grey November, faster near a radiator, faster in a terracotta pot. Water by the calendar and you'll drown the plant one month and parch it the next.

Timer Waters every N days

Ignores weather, season, pot size and plant type. Keeps watering soggy soil.

Wick Waters constantly

Keeps the soil wet whether the plant wants it or not. Fine for ferns, fatal for a cactus.

GreenGenius Waters on evidence

Reads two soil probes, compares them with your plant's moisture band, and only then pumps water.

How it works

A closed loop, not a timer.

Every watering decision starts with a measurement and ends with one.

01 / Measure

Read the soil

Two capacitive probes report moisture from different spots in the root zone, alongside light, temperature and humidity.

02 / Compare

Check the band

Readings are compared with your plant's moisture band, scaled to this pot's own wet and dry points.

03 / Water

Pump a dose

When moisture drops below the band, the pump lifts water from the tank to a drip stake in the soil.

04 / Re-check

Confirm it landed

It measures again before deciding anything else, so a slow-draining pot never gets a second dose it didn't need.

What's inside

Everything lives in the pot.

No hub, no hoses across the windowsill. The planter stacks on its own water tank, and the electronics sit in a sealed bay behind the soil.

  • The base is the reservoir. You top it up through a fill port at the front; any water the soil can't hold drains back into it through slots in the pot floor.

  • A 3 V submersible pump sits in a cradle on the tank floor. A tube runs up a channel inside the pot to a drip stake pushed into the soil, so water arrives at the roots, not on the leaves.

  • Capacitive probes measure moisture without exposed metal in the soil, so they don't corrode. Two of them mean one dry pocket can't fool the system.

  • The ESP32 board slides into rails in a bay walled off from the soil. Small windows let light reach the light sensor and show the status LEDs; a vented pocket lets the air sensor read the room.

  • A sensing strip hangs from the bay into the tank. It tells GreenGenius when to ask you for a refill, and stops the pump from ever running dry.

Sensors & hardware

Six parts, one decision.

All of it runs on our own circuit board, designed around an ESP32.

Light

Temperature & humidity

Reads the room air from a vented pocket. Flags cold drafts and dry winter heating.

Water level

Knows how much is left in the tank, asks for a refill in time, and never runs the pump dry.

Resistive level strip

Pump

A small submersible pump, switched by a MOSFET on the board, delivers water to a drip stake in the soil.

3 V submersible · AO3400A driver

The brain

An ESP32 with Wi-Fi runs the loop on the device itself. Power and firmware updates both come in over one USB-C port.

ESP32-WROOM-32E · USB-C
Plant care, as numbers

"Water when the top inch is dry" becomes a threshold.

Care labels are written for people. GreenGenius turns each one into a moisture band measured against your pot's own range, from its dry point to fully watered, so the same advice works in any soil mix.
