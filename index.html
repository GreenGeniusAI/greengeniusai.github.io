The problem

Watering is a timing problem.

Too much and the roots sit wet. Too little and the plant dries out. The right amount changes every week, and nothing on the surface of the pot tells you which way it's going.

01

Soil never dries at the same rate twice.

Light, season, heating, pot size and soil mix all change how fast water leaves a pot. A fixed watering day can't follow any of them.

02

Care advice is written in words.

"Water when the top inch is dry" is sound advice. Checking it by hand, every day, for every plant, is where it falls apart.

03

The damage shows up late.

Wilting and yellowing appear days after the soil went wrong. By the time you see it, the cause is already in the past.

The pot does

The checking.

  1. 01
    MeasuresSoil moisture at two points, plus light, temperature, humidity and reservoir level.
  2. 02
    DecidesCompares moisture with the plant's range, calibrated to this pot's own soil.
  3. 03
    WatersPumps from the reservoir to a drip stake at the roots, then measures again.

You do

Almost nothing.

  1. 01
    Pot the plantPush in the soil probes and the drip stake, and fill the reservoir.
  2. 02
    Choose its care type"Top inch dry", "keep evenly moist" and so on. The pot sets its targets from it.
  3. 03
    Refill when askedThe level sensor tells you before the reservoir runs out.

Engineering

Built from the board up.

GreenGenius is not an off-the-shelf controller in a nicer pot. The circuit board, the sensor layout and the watering logic are our own, so hardware and software can be developed together.

What's defensible

The hard part isn't the pump.

Published plant-care guidance is written in words, and the numeric datasets that exist mostly sit behind restrictive licences.

GreenGenius turns cited care categories into numeric bands for each sensor, calibrated to every pot's own wet and dry readings. That knowledge layer is what makes the hardware useful, and it is ours to build and own.
