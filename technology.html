Technology

What's inside, part by part.

Five sensors, a pump and a board we designed, plus the software that turns plant advice into targets.

Five readings, taken on the pot.

Two capacitive probes measure moisture in separate spots of the root zone, so one dry pocket can't decide for the whole pot. Capacitive sensing has no exposed metal in the soil, avoiding the corrosion that resistive probes suffer.

A light-dependent resistor records brightness through the day, a DHT-series sensor reads air temperature and humidity, and a level strip tracks the reservoir.

Water goes to the roots.

A small submersible pump sits in the reservoir. When the board calls for water it pushes a dose up a silicone tube to a drip stake in the soil, so water reaches the roots, not the leaves.

The pump is switched by a MOSFET on the board. The level sensor is checked before every dose, so the pump never runs dry.

One board runs everything.

GreenGenius runs on its own circuit board, designed in KiCad around an ESP32 module. It reads every sensor, drives the pump and makes watering decisions locally.

It runs from 5 V over USB-C. The same port updates the firmware through a USB-to-serial chip. Two LEDs show power and status; reset and boot buttons sit on the board.

Plant knowledge, and an app.

The care engine turns written care guidance into targets the pot can act on: moisture bands relative to each pot's own range, and light tiers in lux. Change the plant and the targets change with it.

The companion app is in development. It is being built to identify a plant from a photo, look up its care needs, show live readings and flag anything outside the plant's range.
