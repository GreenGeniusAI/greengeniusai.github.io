Technology

What's inside, and why.

GreenGenius combines five sensors, a pump and a custom ESP32 board with a care engine that turns plant advice into measurable targets.

Five readings, taken on the pot.

Two capacitive probes measure soil moisture in separate spots of the root zone, so one dry pocket doesn't decide for the whole pot. Capacitive sensing has no exposed metal in the soil, which avoids the corrosion common with resistive probes.

A light-dependent resistor records brightness through the day. A DHT-series sensor reads air temperature and humidity, and a level strip tracks how much water is left in the reservoir.

Water goes to the roots.

A small submersible pump sits in the reservoir. When the controller calls for water, it pushes a dose up a silicone tube to a drip stake in the soil, so water reaches the roots rather than the leaves.

The pump is switched by a MOSFET on the board. The level sensor lets the controller skip watering when the reservoir is empty, so the pump never runs dry.

One board runs everything.

GreenGenius uses its own circuit board, designed in KiCad around an ESP32-WROOM-32E module. The board reads every sensor, drives the pump and makes watering decisions locally.

It runs from 5 V over USB-C. The same port is used to update firmware, through a CH340C USB-to-serial chip. Two LEDs show power and status, and reset and boot buttons are on the board.

Plant knowledge and an app.

The care engine turns written care guidance into numbers the pot can act on: moisture bands relative to each pot's own range, and light tiers measured in lux. Bands are stored per plant, so changing the plant changes the targets.

The companion app is in development. It is being built to identify a plant from a photo, look up its care requirements, show live readings from the pot and flag conditions outside the plant's range.
