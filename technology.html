How it works

Four sensors, one decision.

Terra One answers a single question, forty-eight times a day: does this soil need water right now, and if so, how much? Everything in the device exists to answer that well.

01 — Sense

The sensor array.

Five channels, sampled every thirty minutes and buffered on-device. Nothing depends on a network connection.

Soil moisture ×2

Capacitive probes at 4 cm and 10 cm. Two depths give a gradient, which is what reveals the drying curve — a single probe only ever gives a point.

Ambient light

Integrates lux over the day, so the app can tell you whether the spot you chose delivers the light your plant's profile actually calls for.

Temp & humidity

A combined sensor at the rim reads the microclimate around the leaves — the input that decides how fast the soil will dry this week.

Reservoir level

The tank reports its own level, so refill reminders arrive about a week ahead rather than the morning the pump runs dry.

02 — The board

Dual-core, and deliberately boring.

Terra One runs on a custom two-layer board built around an ESP32-WROOM-32E — a dual-core 240 MHz part shipping in millions of connected devices. We chose it because it is well understood, second-sourced and cheap to support for seven years, not because it is exciting.

The analog sensor channels sit on a separate ground pour from the radio and the pump driver. That isolation is the difference between stable moisture readings and readings that drift every time the Wi-Fi transmits — the most common failure we found in competing devices we tore down.

03 — Decide

What the model actually does.

"AI" is doing a lot of work in most smart-planter marketing. Here is the specific, unglamorous version of what runs on Terra One.

01

Fits your drying curve

A small regression, trained on-device over the first two weeks, learns how quickly your soil in your room loses moisture at a given temperature and humidity.

02

Waters ahead of stress

Because it can project the curve forward, it doses before the plant crosses its stress threshold rather than after — the difference between a plant surviving and a plant growing.

03

Flags anomalies

Soil draining faster than the fitted model predicts usually means a cracked pot or a root problem. Terra One notices the deviation and tells you what changed.

What it isn't. There is no camera and no microphone in Terra One, and no large language model anywhere in the device. It does not diagnose disease from photographs or identify your plant by looking at it — you tell it the species once. Cloud processing is limited to aggregating trends for the app; the watering decision itself is made on the device and works with the internet unplugged.

04 — Act

Dose, wait, verify.

A 1.2-litre reservoir feeds a diaphragm pump switched by a MOSFET off the main board. A dose is defined in millilitres and delivered by run-time against a calibrated flow rate — typically 30–60 ml for a mid-size plant.

The important part is what happens next. Terra One waits fifteen minutes for the water to move through the column, re-reads both moisture probes, and confirms the soil responded the way it should have. If it didn't, it flags the discrepancy instead of dosing again. That single check is what ended the flooding we saw in earlier revisions.

A replaceable cartridge meters liquid feed into the same flow during the growing season, and can be disabled per plant.
